I keep a curated list of games built mostly with AI. This month I checked every link: **28 of 61 were broken.** Six of those games had moved somewhere else. The other **22 were gone for good.**

So I looked at a bigger sample. Pieter Levels' Vibe Jam publishes an open API with every entry from both editions and whether it still loads:

|                                | Games | Offline today |
| ------------------------------ | ----- | ------------- |
| Vibe Jam 2025                  | 1,083 | **42%**       |
| Vibe Jam 2026 (ended May 2026) | 945   | 13%           |

Four in ten games from a jam with more than 1,000 entries have disappeared within about a year and a half.

## Where a game is hosted predicts whether it survives

| Host          | 2025 games | Offline | 2026 games | Offline |
| ------------- | ---------- | ------- | ---------- | ------- |
| Custom domain | 804        | **50%** | 581        | 14%     |
| vercel.app    | 98         | 14%     | 177        | 7%      |
| netlify.app   | 48         | 15%     | 53         | 4%      |
| github.io     | 61         | 20%     | 35         | 9%      |
| pages.dev     | 8          | 0%      | 37         | 3%      |
| onrender.com  | 27         | 30%     | 14         | 29%     |
| railway.app   | 2          | —       | 14         | **86%** |

Two patterns:

1. **Custom domains lapse.** Half of the 2025 games on their own `.com` or `.io` are offline. Free static subdomains (Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, GitHub Pages) lost 0–20%. A domain is a yearly bill, and a jam game isn't worth renewing for most people.
2. **Servers die, static files don't.** Games that need a running backend on Railway, Render or Fly are dying fastest. 12 of the 14 Railway-hosted 2026 games are already offline after five months. Free trials end and free tiers sleep, while a static bundle on a CDN costs nothing to keep alive.

## What the survivors are built with

- **Three.js is the default engine of vibe coding:** 60% of 2025 entries and 65% of 2026. Phaser holds at 5–6%, and Godot went from 5 games to 21.
- In my curated list, where creators say which AI they used, **Claude appears in 25 of 35 games** (Claude Code in 7). Cursor is in 13, and Codex and Grok in 4 each.

## If you ship a vibe-coded game

- Deploy the static build to a free subdomain host **and** point your custom domain at it. If the domain lapses, the subdomain still works.
- Keep multiplayer optional, so the single-player game survives when the server doesn't.
- Put the source on GitHub, so you or anyone else can redeploy it later.

## Method and caveats

- **Offline status** is the `is_up` flag from vibejam.com/api as of 2026-09-24. I didn't re-check the 2,028 URLs individually.
- **Small samples:** Railway (2025), pages.dev (2025) and Fly.io have too few games to generalize.
- **My own list** was checked by hand (HTTP status, rendered page, and whether the URL still hosts the same game).
